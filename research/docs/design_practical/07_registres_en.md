# Registers

## A door for a whole row of reeds

A register closes or opens a whole set of reeds at once: a slide pierced with a row of holes moves under the reed blocks, and its holes either face the holes of the chambers or not. It is a pallet for a row instead of a note, and it shares the troubles of both pallets and joints. This chapter is short, because nothing has been measured on a register in this work; it says what the physics of the previous parts predicts, so that the first measurements know what to look for.

### Tight and free at once

A slide must do two things that pull against each other. It must seal, so that a closed register stays silent; and it must move, so that the player can change registers in a breath. Sealing asks for a small gap, and the cube law of the leak part is merciless: a slide of 200 mm with a land of 5 mm on each side of its holes, a gap of 0.05 mm, leaks of the order of a litre per minute at 500 Pa. Moving asks for little friction, and friction grows with the force that presses the slide onto its bed: $F_{\mathrm{move}} = \mu N$.

The cube law also says where the compromise lies. Because the leak goes as the cube of the gap, a modest reduction of the gap buys a large reduction of the leak: a gap smaller by a fifth halves the leak. A slide does not need to be crushed onto its bed to seal; it needs a bed that is flat and a light, even pressure, from a spring or a felt, that keeps it there.

### Push and draw are not the same

The slide sits between the air of the bellows and the chambers, and the bellows pushes on it in one direction and pulls on it in the other, exactly as it does on a pallet. On one side of the bellows the pressure presses the slide onto its bed and helps it seal; on the other it lifts it and opens the gap. A register can therefore be tight on the push and whisper on the draw, and must be tested in both directions, like a furnished reed block.

### A closed register that whispers

A closed register that leaks feeds the chambers of its row. Their reeds receive a little air, and those close to their threshold sound faintly behind the others: a ghost voice rather than a ghost note, heard most on the soft notes of the row.

## Making and checking

Check a register the way a block is checked: the case closed, the register shut, the slots of its reeds open, on the gasometer, in pressure and in suction. The flow that disappears when the register is opened and its pallets kept shut is the leak of the slide. The proposed figure for the whole closed case, slides included, is under 0.5 L/min at 500 Pa.

## At the repair bench

**A register that is hard to move.** Swollen wood or a hardened felt: the friction has grown. Look at the season first; a slide that binds in a damp winter may be free in summer, and planing it then makes it leak in the next dry spell.

**A closed register that can be heard.** A leak of the slide: a bed no longer flat, a felt crushed. The cube law again: restore the flatness and the even pressure before adding force.
