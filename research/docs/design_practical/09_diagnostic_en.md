# The repairer's diagnosis

## From the symptom to the piece

An instrument arrives at the bench with a sentence, not with a diagnosis: "it's tiring", "this note doesn't come", "there's a sound when I push hard". Each part of this book ended with the repair of its own piece. This last part goes the other way round, from what the player says to the piece that says it, so that the first quarter of an hour with an open instrument is spent checking, not guessing.

### Listen, then count

Three gestures come before any screwdriver, and none takes more than a few minutes.

**Play it with the player.** Ask which notes, which direction, which nuance. Most faults of an accordion depend on the direction of the bellows and on the pressure, because pallets, valves, slides and joints all see the bellows push in one direction and pull in the other.

**Count the drop test.** A known weight on the bellows, air valve shut, no key: the time of the full drop, push and draw. Under 20 to 30 seconds, there is a frank leak and nothing else should be judged before it is found (the leak part, @tab:drop).

**Pass the tuner over the keyboard.** Deviation, push and draw, and beat of each voice. A single note off among tuned ones points to that reed or to its air; a whole region off points to pressure, leaks or a block.

### The table

Table: From what the player says to the piece to check. {#tab:diagnosis}
| what the player says | likely cause | check | part |
|---|---|---|---|
| it is tiring, the soft notes start badly | global leak | drop test, then each half closed | valves and leaks |
| a note sounds faintly when pushing hard | a pallet lifts: spring too weak for its hole | gram gauge, half a gram per mm² of hole | right-hand keyboard |
| a note sounds faintly, always | pallet leaking, off-centre or hardened leather | lamp, bell | right-hand keyboard |
| a neighbouring note whispers | leak between two chambers under the block | take the block off, look at its face | reed blocks |
| a voice is heard with its register shut | register slide leaking | gasometer, register shut | registers |
| one note late and soft | leak near its reed (wax, valve) | tape the slot, test the block | reed blocks |
| one note silent | reed touching, lift lost, partner's valve missing | lamp, feeler | reeds |
| one note sounds in one direction only | valve missing or stuck | look at the plate | reeds |
| a buzz or a rattle | reed grazing, loose rivet, valve slapping, grille touching | pluck, lamp | reeds, case |
| a heavy button at the start of a draw | the pressure on the closed pallet (normal) | compare with neighbours | right-hand keyboard |
| a button stays down or returns slowly | friction or spring unhooked | key alone, spring off | right-hand keyboard |
| a heavy, grinding bass button | sliding rods, wedging | the left-hand chapters | left-hand mechanism |
| musette tremolo gone on a note | voices too close, locked | tuner, one voice blocked | reeds |
| out of tune between push and draw | the two tongues of the plate disagree | tuner, push and draw apart | reeds |

### What the table does not know

The table is built from the physics of this book and from the booklet of the training courses. It does not yet know the most important thing, which is how often each line happens on a real bench. A diagnosis starts with the likeliest cause, and only experience says which that is.
