# Case, grille and straps

## The box that holds it all

The case carries everything: the soundboard, the blocks, the mechanism, the bellows frame, the straps, and the hands. On the instruments of the training courses its sides are milled before the course and glued by the trainee in a gluing jig, dry-assembled first; the button board, its cap and the stiffeners follow, then levelling and sanding, varnish or oil. What the box and the grille do to the sound, a filter for the grille, a cavity for a resonance box, is acoustics and belongs to the thesis (chapter on timbral elements). This chapter keeps what decides whether the box is right for the maker: that it is tight, that it stays true through the seasons, and that the hands are well held.

### Wood moves, joints open

Wood is not a stable material, and the accordion is a box of joints. Between green and dry, wood shrinks of the order of 5 to 10 per cent across the grain and of the order of 0.1 to 0.2 per cent along it (Wood Handbook of the Forest Products Laboratory, orders of magnitude): across the grain it moves a few tens of times more than along it. A case is glued from pieces whose grains cross, so every change of humidity makes some joints work. A joint that worked last winter is a gap this summer, and the cube law of the leak part says what a gap of a few hundredths of a millimetre costs. This is the physical reason behind the advice of the booklet: keep the instrument in a dry place at a steady temperature, away from the damp cellar and the attic, and avoid sudden changes, a hot car then a cold room.

It is also the reason for two habits of the workshop: no filler on a gluing surface, so that the glue holds wood to wood; and stiffeners glued flat with no light under them, so that the face they hold stays plane.

### The grille

The grille is the perforated screen in front of the right-hand pallets. For the maker it has one rule: it must touch neither the mechanism nor a button pressed down, otherwise it rubs, clicks, or holds a key down. Check it by pressing every button with the grille in place. For the sound, the grille is a filter whose cut-off depends on its open area and its depth; the thesis says how to measure it, by recording one note with and without the grille.

### Straps

The straps hold the instrument; the left-hand strap holds the hand against the bellows. On the training courses they are fixed on a support milled in the right-hand case and adjusted to the player, sitting and then standing. The booklet's check is the right one, and it is ergonomic, not acoustic: the instrument is stable sitting and standing, and the left thumb touches no screw.

## Making and checking

**Case glued dry first.** Assemble in the jig without glue; anything that does not close dry will not close glued. Then glue the four sides, check square and flat.

**Closed case, leak checked.** Close the case with a board in place of the bellows, on the gasometer, and pass the smoke along the joints, the frame, the mechanism and the register knobs. Proposed figure: under 0.5 L/min at 500 Pa.

## At the repair bench

**A case that leaks after a change of season.** A joint that has opened. Smoke finds it; a seam of glue or, on a frame, a new gasket closes it. Plane only when the wood has settled to the humidity it will live in.

**A grille that clicks or holds a button.** It touches. Look for the shine of a rub on its inner face.
