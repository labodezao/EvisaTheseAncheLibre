# How to read this book

## From the piece to the instrument

The thesis that goes with this book asks how a free reed makes its sound, and what the air around it does to that sound. This book asks a humbler question, the one a maker asks at the bench and a repairer asks with an instrument open in front of him or her: what does this piece do, why is it made the way it is, how do I know it is right, and what do I do when it is not?

An accordion is a box of pressure with doors in it. The bellows makes the pressure, the reeds turn a little of it into sound, the pallets decide which reeds get it, the keyboard opens the pallets, and every joint between two pieces of wood, card, leather or wax is a place where the air may leave without singing. Each part of this book takes one of these pieces and follows the same four steps. First, what physics says about it, kept to the few laws that really decide its behaviour. Then the analysis of a real instrument with those laws: which numbers matter, which do not. Then the tools for the maker, rules of thumb that come out of the laws, with the calculation behind them. And last, the repair bench: the symptoms a player brings, what they point to, and the gesture that checks it.

The acoustics proper, the tongue, its chamber, the coupling of two voices and the sound radiated, belongs to the thesis and is only referred to here. What this book keeps is the physics of making: forces, flows, leaks, stiffness, friction, wood that moves.

Two habits run through every part. The first is to say, each time, where a number comes from: measured, computed, taken from the workshop practice of Ewen and his process sheets, or proposed and still to be checked. Many figures in these pages are of the last kind, and they say so. The second is that every calculation can be redone with the true dimensions of the instrument on the bench. The small program outils_atelier.py (research/scripts) does all the arithmetic of this book, from the leak of a hole to the spring of a pallet, and its tests check that it agrees with the tables printed here.
